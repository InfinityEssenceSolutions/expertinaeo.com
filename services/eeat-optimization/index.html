The discipline

E-E-A-T is signal engineering, not virtue signaling

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is the framework answer engines use to evaluate source quality. It is not a single ranking factor — it is a set of signals that, taken together, indicate whether your page should be cited. Signals include author credentials, source citations, transparent business information, organizational authority and external validation.

We audit your existing E-E-A-T signals across your most important pages, identify gaps versus competitors and best practice, and implement the structural changes — author bylines, citation standards, transparency pages, external authority signals — that lift your trust profile. The work is editorial, structural and external.

E-E-A-T is particularly decisive for YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) topics — health, finance, legal, news — where engines apply stricter source quality standards. For these verticals, E-E-A-T work is non-negotiable.

Common E-E-A-T problems

E-E-A-T engagements begin when a business publishes high-quality content that does not earn citations, particularly in YMYL verticals.

What we fix

  • No author bylines — Content is published without bylines, author schema or credentials.
  • No source citations — Claims are made without supporting references or links to authoritative sources.
  • No transparency pages — About, contact, editorial policy and fact-checking pages are weak or missing.
  • No external authority — Your authors and organization have weak external profiles.
  • YMYL underperformance — Your content does not earn citations for health, finance or legal queries.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does E-E-A-T directly affect rankings?
E-E-A-T is a quality framework, not a single ranking factor. Its signals influence which sources are cited in answers, particularly for YMYL topics.
Who should be the author of our content?
Real people with relevant credentials. We do not recommend AI-generated author identities or fictitious personas — these are detected and penalized.
Do I need a Wikipedia page for E-E-A-T?
Not necessarily. Wikipedia presence helps organizational authority but is not required. Many trusted brands have no Wikipedia page.
How long does E-E-A-T work take?
Structural fixes (bylines, transparency pages, schema) take weeks. External authority building (author profiles, external citations) takes months and is ongoing.
Is E-E-A-T only for YMYL?
No — E-E-A-T signals matter for all content. They are simply more decisive for YMYL, where engines apply stricter source quality standards.
