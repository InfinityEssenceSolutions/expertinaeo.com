Business context

The business situation

A landscaping business in Charlotte operating since 2016 with 11 employees, including a horticulturist, 2 crew leads, and 8 landscapers. The business offers lawn maintenance, landscape design and installation, hardscaping, and seasonal cleanups for residential and commercial properties.

The challenge

Initial challenge

Charlotte's humid subtropical climate (USDA zone 7b/8a) creates specific landscaping considerations — fescue vs Bermuda grass, summer drought pressure, fall leaf removal. The business competes against national lawn-care franchises (Trugreen, etc.) and local landscape design firms. Climate-specific content is essential — generic national content fails Charlotte homeowners.

The opportunity

AEO opportunity

Charlotte-specific climate content (zone 7b/8a plant recommendations, Piedmont clay soil considerations) is differentiated. Cost-transparency content for maintenance and projects is highly citable. Seasonal cadence content (spring cleanup, summer drought, fall leaves, winter prep) compounds authority. Design-principle content with "when to hire a professional" boundaries builds trust.

Baseline audit

Website and content audit findings

A baseline AEO Checker scan would show: AEO Readiness Score approximately 38/100. Critical gaps: no Charlotte-specific climate content, no cost-transparency content, no seasonal cadence content, no LocalBusiness schema on service-area pages, no FAQPage schema. Strengths: 8+ years of operation, horticulturist on staff, Google Business Profile with 100+ reviews averaging 4.7 stars.

Note on baseline scores. The AEO Readiness Score referenced above is an estimated baseline based on typical industry patterns. An actual engagement begins with a real instrumented baseline measurement using our AEO Checker methodology.
Strategy

Proposed strategy

Three-phase strategy: (1) Climate-specific content — Charlotte zone 7b/8a plant content, Piedmont clay soil content, fescue vs Bermuda grass content. (2) Cost and seasonal content — maintenance and project cost ranges, seasonal cadence content calendar. (3) Design and maintenance content — design-principle content with "when to hire a professional" boundaries, maintenance subscription content.

Implementation

Implementation work

Month 1: Baseline measurement, LocalBusiness schema across 5 service-area pages, FAQ content for top 15 Charlotte landscaping queries. Month 2: Charlotte climate-specific content (8 articles), cost-transparency content (6 articles). Month 3: Seasonal cadence content (8 articles), design-principle content (5 articles), monthly performance report.

Timeline

Implementation timeline

3 months minimum engagement. Phase 1 (climate-specific) weeks 1-4. Phase 2 (cost and seasonal) weeks 5-8. Phase 3 (design and maintenance) weeks 9-12 with ongoing quarterly refresh and pre-season content cadence.

Measurement

Measurement methodology

Baseline measurement: AEO Readiness Score, climate-specific-plant snippet share, cost-query AI-answer citation frequency, seasonal-query visibility, local PAA capture. Re-measurement at months 1, 2, 3. Methodology disclosed; attribution discipline applied.

Honest measurement commitment. We do not present fabricated before-and-after metrics, AI citations, rankings or revenue figures. Real engagements report against an instrumented baseline with methodology disclosed and attribution discipline applied.
Proposed takeaways

Key takeaways (proposed, not measured)

These takeaways represent the proposed strategic direction for this type of engagement. They are NOT measured outcomes from a completed client engagement.

  • Charlotte-specific climate content (zone 7b/8a, Piedmont clay soil) differentiates from generic national content and is highly citable.
  • Seasonal cadence content (spring cleanup, summer drought, fall leaves, winter prep) compounds authority over time and is essential for landscaping AEO.
  • Cost-transparency content for maintenance and project types is among the highest AI-citation opportunities in landscaping AEO.
  • Design-principle content with "when to hire a professional" boundaries builds trust without giving away the business.
