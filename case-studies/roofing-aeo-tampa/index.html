Business context

The business situation

A roofing business serving the Tampa Bay area since 2014, operating with 22 roofers and 6 project managers. The business specializes in storm damage, insurance claims, and roof replacement for residential properties in Florida's hurricane-prone Gulf Coast.

The challenge

Initial challenge

Tampa is in a high-risk hurricane zone — annual storm pressure creates predictable surge demand for storm-damage roofing content. The business competes against national roofing chains and storm-chasing contractors. Insurance content is complex (Florida-specific) and requires proper compliance review. Without pre-engineered storm-response content, the business misses surge demand after weather events.

The opportunity

AEO opportunity

Tampa-specific storm-content (hurricane damage, tropical storm damage) is highly differentiated. Insurance-claim content with Florida-specific considerations is winnable. Material-comparison content for Florida climate (tile vs asphalt vs metal for hurricane resistance) is high-value. Storm-response content templates pre-engineered for rapid deployment capture surge demand.

Baseline audit

Website and content audit findings

A baseline AEO Checker scan would show: AEO Readiness Score approximately 33/100. Critical gaps: no storm-response content, no Florida-specific insurance content, no material-comparison content for hurricane climate, no LocalBusiness schema on service-area pages, no FAQPage schema. Strengths: 10+ years in Tampa market, strong Google Business Profile with 180+ reviews.

Note on baseline scores. The AEO Readiness Score referenced above is an estimated baseline based on typical industry patterns. An actual engagement begins with a real instrumented baseline measurement using our AEO Checker methodology.
Strategy

Proposed strategy

Three-phase strategy: (1) Storm-response content — pre-engineered templates for hurricane, tropical storm, tree damage; Florida-specific insurance content with proper compliance review. (2) Material and cost content — hurricane-resistance material comparison, cost-transparency content for roof replacement. (3) Inspection content — diagnostic content with clear "when to call a professional" boundaries.

Implementation

Implementation work

Month 1: Baseline measurement, LocalBusiness schema across 6 service-area pages, FAQ content for top 15 Tampa roofing queries. Month 2: Storm-response content templates (4 templates), Florida insurance content (6 articles with compliance review), material-comparison content. Month 3: Cost-transparency content, inspection content library (12 articles), monthly performance report.

Timeline

Implementation timeline

3 months minimum engagement. Phase 1 (storm-response) weeks 1-4. Phase 2 (material and cost) weeks 5-8. Phase 3 (inspection) weeks 9-12 with ongoing quarterly refresh and pre-hurricane-season content refresh.

Measurement

Measurement methodology

Baseline measurement: AEO Readiness Score, storm-damage query visibility, insurance-claim AI-answer citation frequency, cost-query snippet share, material-comparison citation rate. Re-measurement at months 1, 2, 3. Methodology disclosed; attribution discipline applied. All insurance content reviewed by compliance team.

Honest measurement commitment. We do not present fabricated before-and-after metrics, AI citations, rankings or revenue figures. Real engagements report against an instrumented baseline with methodology disclosed and attribution discipline applied.
Proposed takeaways

Key takeaways (proposed, not measured)

These takeaways represent the proposed strategic direction for this type of engagement. They are NOT measured outcomes from a completed client engagement.

  • Pre-engineering storm-response content templates is essential for roofing businesses in hurricane-prone areas — content created after weather events is too late.
  • State-specific insurance content (Florida) is winnable but requires proper compliance review and honest disclaimers.
  • Hurricane-resistance material comparison content is high-value and differentiated from generic national content.
  • Storm-chasing contractors compete on speed; established local roofers compete on authority and trust — content engineering reinforces the latter.
